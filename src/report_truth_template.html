Can a model stop knowing the answer?

"Can LLMs be confused on purpose, consistently making the specific mistake a given misconception produces, instead of slipping into the correct answer or making a random wrong one?" (the project's original question)

The main report asks how often a model makes the tagged mistake. This page asks what happens the rest of the time. Is the model pulled back to the answer it knows is right? Or does it give up the right answer and land on the wrong mistake?

What this says about LLMs

Caveats

  • There's no human baseline. Real students who hold a misconception don't make the matching mistake every time either, so 100% isn't the right ceiling.
  • Eedi's tags are taken as ground truth. Some "another wrong option" picks may also fit the persona's misconception, so the wrong-mistake share is an upper bound.
  • Answers are one letter, with no working shown. A model can't step through the faulty procedure before answering. Letting it "work it wrong" first is the obvious next test.
  • Section 5's distractor temptation comes from the same models' control replies. It describes the data; it isn't an independent measure.
